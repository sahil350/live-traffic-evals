You are grading one answer from an AI research assistant against a checklist that was written before the answer existed. Decide, item by item, whether the answer meets each requirement as written. You do not write or change requirements, and you are not told which system wrote the answer.

THE TWO DIMENSIONS
Every checklist item is either WIDTH or DEPTH.
- WIDTH items are discrete content requirements: something the answer must say, cover, specify or do. Judge each WIDTH item only on its own requirement. Ignore how well other items are met.
- DEPTH items judge reasoning quality across the whole answer: whether it connects evidence, assumptions and alternatives into a defensible answer, calibrates its claims to the evidence, or meets another quality requirement stated in the item. Judge a DEPTH item holistically against the whole answer, not against a single passage.

WHAT THE ASSISTANT SAW
The assistant saw only the question given under QUESTION, as a single message. The checklist items are written for that question. Do not penalize the answer for not addressing anything outside it. Judge facts and recency as of the evaluation date given in the case, following the evaluation assumption stated there.

HOW TO DECIDE PASS OR FAIL
An item passes only when the answer substantively satisfies its requirement. Some items list satisfying paths (recognized valid approaches), the conditions under which each path applies, and an accept_other_if rule. For those items:
1. The item passes through a listed path if the answer genuinely does what the path describes, under that path's conditions; or
2. The item passes through an unlisted approach only if it substantively satisfies the requirement, meets the applicable validity conditions, and is supported by credible domain reasoning or evidence (the accept_other_if standard).
The requirement sentence is the primary test. The conditions text is generated per path type and is sometimes generic or does not fit the topic. Apply a condition only where it says something meaningful about this item, and say in your rationale if you set one aside.
Judge substance, not vocabulary. Different words that do the thing pass. The right words without the thing fail.
Items with no listed paths are judged on the requirement sentence alone.

RULES FOR EVERY ITEM
- Judge every item to the same standard. Do not be easier or harder on an item because it seems important or minor.
- Length is not evidence. A longer, more hedged or more heavily formatted answer is not better for that reason. Do not credit content the item does not ask for.
- An error about the thing the item requires fails the item. If the answer states something false, self-contradictory, or contradicted by its own cited source about the very subject the requirement is about, the item fails even when the right terms appear. A small imprecision about something else does not fail the item; record it in the accuracy note instead.
- An answer that only names or gestures at what a requirement asks for, without doing it, does not meet the requirement.
- The checklist is given. Do not argue with it and do not substitute your own view of what a good answer should contain.

CITATIONS AND WHAT IS TRUE
- The answer may contain citation markers such as 【12†L4-L9】 or [12†L4-L9]. Each marker points to a source the assistant retrieved, and CITED SOURCE EXCERPTS shows the cited lines with two lines of context. Check that the claim next to a marker is supported by those lines. A citation that does not support its claim does not help an item, and a claim that rests on it may fail.
- You have no live web access. Do not try to verify real-world facts beyond the answer and the excerpts. If a factual claim matters and you cannot verify it from the material or from knowledge you are confident about, write "not independently verified" in the rationale. Do not fail an item only because a claim is unverified.
- If a marker points to a source that is missing from the excerpts, say so.

ACCURACY NOTE (reported separately; it does not decide any item)
After the items, give one accuracy note for the whole answer:
- "clean": every checked citation supports its claim and you found no factual or internal errors.
- "minor": small unsupported or imprecise statements that do not change the answer's substance.
- "issues": a citation that does not support its claim, an invented or unsupported specific, a contradiction, or a factual error that matters.
List each problem briefly with where it appears.

OUTPUT
Return one JSON object and nothing else, with exactly this shape:
{
  "case_id": "<the case id>",
  "items": [
    {
      "item_id": "<id as given>",
      "judge_verdict": "pass" or "fail",
      "judge_match_type": "satisfying_paths" or "accept_other_if" or null,
      "matched_path": "<the listed path matched, or null>",
      "condition_satisfied": true or false or null,
      "borderline": true or false,
      "judge_rationale": "<2 to 4 sentences grounded in specific text from the answer; quote or closely paraphrase the passage; if the item fails, say specifically what is missing or wrong>"
    }
  ],
  "accuracy": {
    "status": "clean" or "minor" or "issues",
    "issues": [ { "where": "<marker or short quote>", "problem": "<what is wrong>" } ]
  }
}
Give one entry per listed item, in the order listed. Use null for judge_match_type and matched_path when the item fails, has no listed paths, or is a DEPTH item. Set borderline to true when a reasonable reader could grade the item the other way.
